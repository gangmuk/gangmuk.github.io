Planning LLM inference across clouds and GPU types means choosing deployments no one has run before. Koi treats every deployment it runs as an experiment that explains why it performed as it did, and uses that answer for every job it plans next.

Results

Heterogeneous cluster: 104 GPUs, three regions, three hours

H100, A100 and RTX PRO 6000 GPUs in three Google Cloud regions; 25 online and batch workloads over ten models. Every planner gets the same snapshots, catalog, checks and language model.

Ablation: goodput (tok/s)

1.18× the goodput of the strongest baseline (OpenEvolve) at 20% lower cost per token. Goodput counts only output that meets its SLO. The ablation is a 3-hour simulation: a GPT-5.6 planner with no tools and no memory across ticks gets 71% less goodput than Koi. Koi loses 44% when it stops learning from new runs, and 39% without its causal graph. In 400- and 800-GPU simulations, Koi improves goodput by up to 45% and lowers cost per token by up to 31%.

3 · Why it is hard

One job, six deployments

How a job is deployed decides its throughput and cost, for a given workload (here, 2,048-token prompts and 512-token outputs). A planner must choose this for every job at once, mostly for deployments it has never run. (Yes, the A10G is an old GPU, but the same idea holds on newer GPUs too!)
